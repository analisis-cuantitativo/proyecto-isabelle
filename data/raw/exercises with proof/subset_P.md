if M is a set such that $A \subseteq M$ and $B \subseteq M$, then $A \cup B \subseteq M$

Proof: let $x$ be an arbitrary element. If $x \in A \cup B$, then $x \in A$ or $x \in B$, without loss of generality ,suppose $x \in A$, then by the hypothesis $A \subseteq M$ it follows that $x \in M$, analogously if $x \in B$, the hypotesis $B \subseteq M$ implies $x \in M$, in the both cases, we conclude that $x \in M$, therefore, $A \cup B \subseteq M$
