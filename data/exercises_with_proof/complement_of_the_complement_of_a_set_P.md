Let $A$ be a subset of a Universal set $R$ , then $(A^{c})^{c} = A$

Proof: $\Rightarrow$) If $x \in (A^{c})^{c}$, then $x \in R-(R-A)$, that is, $ x \in R$ and $ x \notin R-A$. Clearly, we cannot have $x \in R$ and $ x \notin R$, so we must have $x \in R$ and $ x \in A$, in particular, $x \in A$ and with this, $(A^{c})^{c} \subseteq A$.

$\Leftarrow$) by contradiction, suppose there exists $y \in A$ such that $y \notin (A^{c})^{c}$, which is equivalent to having, $y \notin R$ or $y \in R-A$.

We cannot have $ y \in A$ and $ y \notin R$, so we must have $y \in A$ and $ y \in R-A$, that means, $y \in A$ and $ y\in R$ and $ y \notin A$, which is a contradiction.

We conclude that $A \subseteq (A^{c})^{c}$, therefore $(A^{c})^{c} = A$
